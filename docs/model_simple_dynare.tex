\section{Model 2: Baseline with Capital}
\label{sec:model2-clean}

This document defines the stationary nonlinear New Keynesian model with capital
implemented in \texttt{models/nk\_balanced\_growth.mod}.

\subsection{Variables}

The endogenous variables are itemized in the following order:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item $r_t^k$: real rental rate of capital,
  \item $w_t$: real wage rate,
  \item $y_t$: aggregate output before price-adjustment costs,
  \item $n_t$: labor hours,
  \item $c_t$: household consumption,
  \item $i_t$: investment,
  \item $k_t$: capital available at the beginning of period $t$,
  \item $\pi_t=p_t/p_{t-1}$: gross inflation rate,
  \item $r_t$: gross nominal interest rate,
  \item $\Psi_t$: real marginal cost,
  \item $s_t$: gross risk-premium wedge on nominal-bond saving,
  \item $\widehat\gamma_t$: log deviation of gross technology growth from its
        balanced-growth value.
\end{enumerate}

Additional variables and objects appearing in the model are
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item $b_t$: real value of a one-period nominal bond,
  \item $d_t$: real profits distributed by intermediate-goods firms,
  \item $p_t(f)$ and $y_t(f)$: the price and output of intermediate firm $f$,
  \item $E_t[\cdot]$: expectation conditional on information available in period $t$,
  \item $\lambda_{t,t+1}$: the household's one-period real stochastic discount
        factor.
\end{itemize}

The exogenous innovations are
\begin{equation*}
  \varepsilon_t^\Gamma \sim \mathbb{N}(0,1),
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_t^s \sim \mathbb{N}(0,1),
\end{equation*}
which enter technology growth and the risk-premium process with standard
deviations $\sigma_\Gamma$ and $\sigma_s$, respectively.

\subsection{Balanced-Growth Normalization}
\label{sec:bgp-normalization}

Following Eusepi and Preston (2011), let $X_t$ denote the labor-augmenting
stochastic trend.  Uppercase letters denote nonstationary levels and
lowercase letters their stationary normalizations.  The growing real quantities
are defined by
\begin{align}
  (Y_t,C_t,I_t,W_t) & =X_t(y_t,c_t,i_t,w_t), \notag \\
  (Adj_t,D_t,B_t)   & =X_t(adj_t,d_t,b_t),
  \label{eq:growing-normalizations}                 \\
  K_t               & =X_{t-1}k_t.
  \label{eq:capital-normalization}
\end{align}
Hours, the real capital rental rate, gross inflation, the gross nominal rate,
and real marginal cost are stationary without division by $X_t$:
$n_t=N_t$, $r_t^k=R_t^k$, $\pi_t=\Pi_t$, $r_t=R_t$, and $\Psi_t$ retains its
notation.  Here ``stationary'' means stationary around a constant
balanced-growth steady state, not constant period by period.

As in Eusepi and Preston, $K_t$ is predetermined capital available at the
beginning of period $t$, and $k_t=K_t/X_{t-1}$.  Investment chosen in period $t$
determines $K_{t+1}$.  This is a reindexing of the original document's
end-of-period convention, not a change in the underlying accumulation technology.

Let $\Gamma_t$ denote gross technology growth, $\gamma_t$ its logarithm, and
$\widehat\gamma_t$ the log deviation of growth from its balanced-growth value.
The stochastic trend and growth process satisfy
\begin{align}
  X_t & = \Gamma_t X_{t-1},
  \qquad \gamma_t\equiv\log\Gamma_t,
  \label{eq:trend-level}                 \\
  \widehat\gamma_t
  \equiv\gamma_t-\log\bar\Gamma
      & =\rho_\Gamma\widehat\gamma_{t-1}
  +\sigma_\Gamma\varepsilon_t^\Gamma,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_t^\Gamma\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
  \quad 0\leq\rho_\Gamma<1.
  \label{eq:trend-growth}
\end{align}
The Eusepi--Preston-comparable baseline sets $\rho_\Gamma=0$, so deviations of
technology growth from $\bar\Gamma$ are i.i.d.  Retaining $\rho_\Gamma$ as an
explicit parameter permits sensitivity exercises without changing the
model equations.  The level $X_t$ nevertheless has a stochastic trend because
each growth realization permanently changes its path.

As in Eusepi and Preston, households observe $X_t$ and the distribution of its
growth process.  Since $X_{t-1}$ is already known, they also observe the realized
$\Gamma_t$.  Observability does not require including $\Gamma_t$ in every
perceived-law-of-motion regression; that information-set choice is stated
separately in the MATLAB learning specification.

Output targets must be expressed in stationary units before entering the policy
rule.  The preference discount factor $\beta$ is constant; the stochastic
discount factor also varies with normalized consumption and technology growth.

\subsection{Parameters}

\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=3.2cm,font=\normalfont]
  \item[$\beta$] household discount factor;
  \item[$\chi$] scale parameter on the disutility of labor;
  \item[$\eta$] inverse of the Frisch elasticity of labor supply, so the Frisch elasticity is $1/\eta$;
  \item[$\delta$] capital depreciation rate;
  \item[$\alpha$] cost share of capital in production;
  \item[$\theta$] elasticity of substitution between differentiated intermediate goods;
  \item[$\varphi$] Rotemberg price-adjustment cost coefficient;
  \item[$\bar{\pi}$] steady-state gross inflation rate;
  \item[$\phi_{\pi}$] monetary-policy response to inflation;
  \item[$\phi_y$] monetary-policy response to output;
  \item[$r^*$] intercept in the monetary-policy rule;
  \item[$\pi^*$] inflation target, with $\pi^*=\bar{\pi}$;
  \item[$y_t^*$] stationary output target, set to steady-state output $\bar y$
        in the baseline;
  \item[$\bar\Gamma$] balanced-growth value of gross technology growth;
  \item[$\rho_\Gamma$] persistence of the log deviation of technology growth,
        with $0\leq\rho_\Gamma<1$;
  \item[$\sigma_\Gamma$] standard deviation of the log technology-growth
        innovation;
  \item[$\bar s$] steady-state gross risk-premium wedge;
  \item[$\rho_s$] persistence of the risk-premium wedge, with
        $0\leq\rho_s<1$;
  \item[$\sigma_s$] standard deviation of the risk-premium innovation.
\end{description}

The calibration retains the original NK values where they remain applicable and
uses the Eusepi--Preston deterministic growth rate for the rewritten technology
process:
\begin{align*}
  \beta  & = 0.995,
         & \frac{1}{\eta} & = 3,
         & \delta         & = 0.025,
         & \alpha         & = 0.33,         \\
  \theta & = 6,
         & \varphi        & = 59.11,
         & \bar\pi        & = 1.006,
         & \phi_\pi       & = 1.5,          \\
  \phi_y & = 0.1,
         & \bar\Gamma     & = \exp(0.0053),
         & \rho_\Gamma    & = 0,
         & \bar s         & = 1,            \\
  \rho_s & = 0,
         & \sigma_s       & = 0.
\end{align*}
The parameter $\chi$ is calibrated so that steady-state labor equals $1/3$.
The value of $\sigma_\Gamma$ is set by the experiment configuration so
that the NK and Eusepi--Preston models receive the same technology-growth
innovation in the same units.
Thus the default has $s_t=1$ in every period and no risk-premium shock.  A
demand-shock experiment must explicitly override $\rho_s$ and $\sigma_s$.

\section{Economic Primitives}

\paragraph{Household objective.}
The representative household chooses $\{c_t,i_t,n_t,b_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}$ to
maximize
\begin{equation}
  E_0\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\beta^t
  \left[
  \log c_t-\frac{\chi n_t^{1+\eta}}{1+\eta}
  \right].
  \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
This stationary objective differs from level utility by the exogenous term
$\log X_t$, which does not affect the household's choices.

\paragraph{Household budget constraint.}
In levels, consumption, investment, and bond purchases equal labor income,
capital income, the real payoff from previously purchased nominal bonds, and
firm profits:
\begin{equation}
  C_t+I_t+B_t
  =W_tN_t+R_t^kK_t
  +\frac{R_{t-1}B_{t-1}}{\Pi_t}+D_t.
  \label{eq:budget-level}
\end{equation}
Here $B_t$ is the real value of the nominal bond purchased in period $t$, and
$R_{t-1}B_{t-1}/\Pi_t$ is its realized real payoff.  Divide by $X_t$ and use
$b_t=B_t/X_t$, $k_t=K_t/X_{t-1}$, and
$b_{t-1}=B_{t-1}/X_{t-1}$.  The stationary budget constraint is
\begin{equation}
  c_t+i_t+b_t
  =w_tn_t+r_t^k\frac{k_t}{\Gamma_t}
  +\frac{r_{t-1}b_{t-1}}{\pi_t\Gamma_t}+d_t.
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Both predetermined stocks are divided by $\Gamma_t$ because they were chosen or
measured relative to $X_{t-1}$ but the period-$t$ budget is normalized by
$X_t$.

\paragraph{Capital accumulation.}
In levels, next period's capital equals undepreciated beginning-of-period
capital plus current investment:
\begin{equation}
  K_{t+1}=(1-\delta)K_t+I_t.
  \label{eq:capital-law-level}
\end{equation}
Divide by $X_t$ and use $k_{t+1}=K_{t+1}/X_t$,
$k_t=K_t/X_{t-1}$, $i_t=I_t/X_t$, and
$\Gamma_t=X_t/X_{t-1}$.  The stationary law of motion is therefore
\begin{equation}
  k_{t+1}=\frac{1-\delta}{\Gamma_t}k_t+i_t.
  \label{eq:capital-law}
\end{equation}
Technology growth divides the surviving stock because $k_t$ is normalized by
$X_{t-1}$ whereas $k_{t+1}$ is normalized by $X_t$.

\paragraph{Intermediate-goods production.}
Each intermediate firm combines beginning-of-period capital and current labor
with labor-augmenting technology:
\begin{equation}
  Y_t(f)=K_t(f)^{\alpha}\left[X_tN_t(f)\right]^{1-\alpha}.
  \label{eq:firm-production}
\end{equation}
In a symmetric equilibrium, this becomes
\begin{equation}
  Y_t=K_t^{\alpha}(X_tN_t)^{1-\alpha}.
  \label{eq:aggregate-production}
\end{equation}
Using $y_t=Y_t/X_t$, $k_t=K_t/X_{t-1}$,
$n_t=N_t$, and $\Gamma_t=X_t/X_{t-1}$ gives
\begin{align}
  y_t
   & =\frac{K_t^{\alpha}(X_tN_t)^{1-\alpha}}{X_t} \notag  \\
   & =\left(\frac{K_t}{X_t}\right)^{\alpha}N_t^{1-\alpha}
  =\left(\frac{k_t}{\Gamma_t}\right)^{\alpha}n_t^{1-\alpha}.
  \label{eq:stationary-production}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Technology process.}
The nonstationary technology level grows according to the stationary process
defined in \eqref{eq:trend-level}--\eqref{eq:trend-growth}:
\begin{equation}
  \log\Gamma_t-\log\bar\Gamma
  =\rho_\Gamma(\log\Gamma_{t-1}-\log\bar\Gamma)
  +\sigma_\Gamma\varepsilon_t^\Gamma.
  \label{eq:technology}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Final-good aggregation.}
The representative final-goods firm combines differentiated intermediate goods with a Dixit--Stiglitz aggregator:
\begin{equation}
  y_t
  \equiv
  \left[
    \int_0^1 y_t(f)^{(\theta-1)/\theta}\,df
    \right]^{\theta/(\theta-1)}.
  \label{eq:final-good}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Demand for an intermediate good.}
Cost minimization by the final-goods firm yields demand for firm $f$'s output:
\begin{equation}
  y_t(f)=\left(\frac{p_t(f)}{p_t}\right)^{-\theta}y_t.
  \label{eq:firm-demand}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Aggregate price index.}
The price of the final good is the corresponding Dixit--Stiglitz price index:
\begin{equation}
  p_t
  =\left[
    \int_0^1 p_t(f)^{1-\theta}\,df
    \right]^{1/(1-\theta)}.
  \label{eq:price-index}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rotemberg price-adjustment cost.}
Each intermediate firm loses output when it changes its price relative to steady-state inflation:
\begin{equation}
  adj_t(f)
  =\frac{\varphi}{2}
  \left[
    \frac{p_t(f)}{\bar\pi p_{t-1}(f)}-1
    \right]^2y_t.
  \label{eq:adjustment-cost-firm}
\end{equation}
In a symmetric equilibrium,
\begin{equation}
  adj_t
  =\frac{\varphi}{2}
  \left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}-1\right)^2y_t.
  \label{eq:adjustment-cost}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Pricing kernel.}
With log utility, the household's one-period real stochastic discount factor in
levels is $\beta C_t/C_{t+1}$.  Substituting $C_t=X_tc_t$ and
$C_{t+1}=\Gamma_{t+1}X_tc_{t+1}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{t,t+1}
  =\beta\frac{c_t}{\Gamma_{t+1}c_{t+1}},
  \label{eq:pricing-kernel-one}
\end{equation}

\section{Equilibrium Equation Block}

The following equations are the compact equilibrium system most directly corresponding to a Dynare model.

\paragraph{Household labor-supply condition.}
The real wage equals the household's marginal rate of substitution between consumption and labor:
\begin{equation}
  w_t=\chi n_t^{\eta}c_t.
  \label{eq:labor-foc}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Nominal-bond Euler equation.}
The household is indifferent at the margin between current consumption and
purchasing a one-period nominal bond.  Using the stationary pricing kernel,
\begin{equation}
  1
  =s_tr_tE_t\left[
  \beta\frac{c_t}{\Gamma_{t+1}c_{t+1}}
  \frac{1}{\pi_{t+1}}
  \right].
  \label{eq:bond-euler}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $1=s_tr_tE_t[\lambda_{t,t+1}/\pi_{t+1}]$.  The growth factor is
part of the stochastic discount factor; it is not an additional return on the
bond.  A positive innovation that raises $s_t$ increases the effective return
to saving at a given policy rate and therefore acts as a contractionary demand
shock.

\paragraph{Risk-premium process.}
Following the additive AR(1) specification in the cited demand-shock model, the
gross risk-premium wedge evolves as
\begin{equation}
  s_t=(1-\rho_s)\bar s+\rho_s s_{t-1}+\sigma_s\varepsilon_t^s.
  \label{eq:risk-premium-block}
\end{equation}
The baseline $\bar s=1$ leaves the deterministic steady state unchanged.  The
additive process is a local approximation and does not impose global positivity;
the shock calibration must therefore keep simulated values of $s_t$ positive.

\paragraph{Capital Euler equation.}
In levels, the marginal utility cost of saving through capital equals the
expected discounted marginal utility of its rental return and undepreciated
value:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{C_t}
  =E_t\left[
    \beta\frac{1}{C_{t+1}}
    \left(R_{t+1}^k+1-\delta\right)
    \right].
  \label{eq:capital-euler-level}
\end{equation}
Substituting $C_t=X_tc_t$,
$C_{t+1}=X_{t+1}c_{t+1}=\Gamma_{t+1}X_tc_{t+1}$, and
$r_{t+1}^k=R_{t+1}^k$ gives the stationary Euler equation
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{c_t}
  =E_t\left[
    \frac{\beta}{\Gamma_{t+1}}\frac{1}{c_{t+1}}
    \left(r_{t+1}^k+1-\delta\right)
    \right].
  \label{eq:capital-euler}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/\Gamma_{t+1}$ converts next period's normalized marginal utility
back into units comparable with period $t$.

\paragraph{Capital-demand condition.}
In levels, the real rental rate of capital equals capital's marginal revenue
product, $R_t^k=\alpha\Psi_tY_t/K_t$.  Because
$Y_t=X_ty_t$, $K_t=X_{t-1}k_t$, and $X_t/X_{t-1}=\Gamma_t$, the stationary
condition is
\begin{equation}
  r_t^k=\alpha\Psi_t\Gamma_t\frac{y_t}{k_t}.
  \label{eq:capital-foc}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Labor-demand condition.}
In levels, labor demand satisfies
$W_t=(1-\alpha)\Psi_tY_t/N_t$.  Dividing the real wage and output by the same
trend $X_t$ leaves no technology-growth factor in the stationary condition:
\begin{equation}
  w_t=(1-\alpha)\Psi_t\frac{y_t}{n_t}.
  \label{eq:firm-labor-foc}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Aggregate production.}
In a symmetric equilibrium, stationary aggregate production is
\begin{equation}
  y_t=\left(\frac{k_t}{\Gamma_t}\right)^{\alpha}n_t^{1-\alpha}.
  \label{eq:production-block}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rotemberg pricing condition.}
A price change today balances the current markup incentive against the expected value of future price adjustment:
\begin{equation}
  \varphi
  \left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}-1\right)
  \frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}
  =(1-\theta)+\theta\Psi_t
  +\varphi E_t\left[
    \lambda_{t,t+1}\Gamma_{t+1}
    \left(\frac{\pi_{t+1}}{\bar\pi}-1\right)
    \frac{\pi_{t+1}}{\bar\pi}
    \frac{y_{t+1}}{y_t}
    \right].
  \label{eq:pricing-foc}
\end{equation}
Here $Y_{t+1}/Y_t=\Gamma_{t+1}y_{t+1}/y_t$.  Consequently, the displayed
$\Gamma_{t+1}$ cancels the $1/\Gamma_{t+1}$ inside
$\lambda_{t,t+1}$; keeping both factors visible records where that cancellation
comes from.

\paragraph{Monetary-policy rule.}
The central bank sets the gross nominal interest rate in response to inflation
and output:
\begin{equation}
  r_t
  =r^*\left(\frac{\pi_t}{\pi^*}\right)^{\phi_\pi}
  \left(\frac{y_t}{y_t^*}\right)^{\phi_y}.
  \label{eq:policy-rule}
\end{equation}
The baseline abstracts from the zero lower bound and therefore keeps the policy
equation smooth around the balanced-growth steady state.
The inflation target is $\pi^*=\bar\pi$.  The baseline output target is the
stationary balanced-growth steady state:
\begin{equation}
  y_t^*=\bar y
  \label{eq:ss-output-target}
\end{equation}
This makes the policy rule respond to stationary output relative to its constant
steady-state benchmark.  An output-gap extension would require deriving the
flexible-price allocation of this capital model.

\paragraph{Capital law of motion.}
Current investment determines next period's capital stock:
\begin{equation}
  k_{t+1}=\frac{1-\delta}{\Gamma_t}k_t+i_t.
  \label{eq:capital-block}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Resource constraint.}
Consumption and investment exhaust output net of Rotemberg price-adjustment costs:
\begin{equation}
  c_t+i_t
  =y_t-
  \frac{\varphi}{2}
  \left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}-1\right)^2y_t.
  \label{eq:resource-direct}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Technology process.}
Technology growth follows the stationary process
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\gamma_t
  =\rho_\Gamma\widehat\gamma_{t-1}
  +\sigma_\Gamma\varepsilon_t^\Gamma.
  \label{eq:technology-block}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Bond-market clearing.}
Because the representative household's bond position is in zero net supply,
\begin{equation}
  b_t=0.
  \label{eq:bond-clearing}
\end{equation}

\section{Balanced-Growth Steady State}

At the deterministic balanced-growth steady state,
$\widehat\gamma=0$, $\Gamma=\bar\Gamma$, $s=\bar s$,
$\pi=\bar\pi$, and every normalized real variable is constant.  The capital
Euler equation then implies
\begin{equation}
  \bar r^k=\frac{\bar\Gamma}{\beta}-(1-\delta).
  \label{eq:ss-capital-return}
\end{equation}
The stationary capital law gives the investment--capital ratio
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\bar i}{\bar k}
  =1-\frac{1-\delta}{\bar\Gamma}.
  \label{eq:ss-investment-capital-ratio}
\end{equation}
Finally, the nominal-bond Euler equation gives
\begin{equation}
  \bar r=\frac{\bar\Gamma\bar\pi}{\beta\bar s}.
  \label{eq:ss-nominal-rate}
\end{equation}
Because the policy targets are met in steady state, the smooth Taylor rule also
requires $r^*=\bar r$.  With the baseline $\bar s=1$, this reduces to
$r^*=\bar\Gamma\bar\pi/\beta$.

At $\bar\pi=\pi^*$, both Rotemberg adjustment terms are zero.  The pricing
condition therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}
  \bar\Psi=\frac{\theta-1}{\theta}.
  \label{eq:ss-marginal-cost}
\end{equation}
Capital demand then determines the capital--output ratio:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\bar k}{\bar y}
  =\frac{\alpha\bar\Psi\bar\Gamma}{\bar r^k}.
  \label{eq:ss-capital-output-ratio}
\end{equation}
Given the target $\bar n$, stationary production determines output as
\begin{equation}
  \bar y
  =\left(
  \frac{\bar k/\bar y}{\bar\Gamma}
  \right)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\bar n.
  \label{eq:ss-output}
\end{equation}
The remaining real allocation follows sequentially from capital accumulation,
the resource constraint, and labor demand:
\begin{align}
  \bar k
         & =\left(\frac{\bar k}{\bar y}\right)\bar y,
  \label{eq:ss-capital}                                       \\
  \bar i
         & =\left[1-\frac{1-\delta}{\bar\Gamma}\right]\bar k,
  \label{eq:ss-investment}                                    \\
  \bar c & =\bar y-\bar i,
  \label{eq:ss-consumption}                                   \\
  \bar w & =(1-\alpha)\bar\Psi\frac{\bar y}{\bar n}.
  \label{eq:ss-wage}
\end{align}
Finally, if labor is calibrated to $\bar n=1/3$, the labor-supply condition
determines the preference scale rather than requiring a nonlinear solve:
\begin{equation}
  \chi=\frac{\bar w}{\bar n^{\eta}\bar c}.
  \label{eq:ss-labor-disutility-scale}
\end{equation}
These relationships are algebraic evaluations of the stationary equilibrium
conditions.  Dynare can calculate their numerical values and then verify the
residuals of the full nonlinear model.

\section{Compact Dynare Ordering}

For direct transcription into Dynare, define the model-local gross growth factor
\begin{equation*}
  \Gamma_t=\bar\Gamma\exp(\widehat\gamma_t).
\end{equation*}
The twelve endogenous variables are then determined by the following twelve
equations, in declaration order:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item Capital demand:
        \begin{equation*}
          r_t^k=\alpha\Psi_t\Gamma_t\frac{y_t}{k_t}.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Household labor supply:
        \begin{equation*}
          w_t=\chi n_t^\eta c_t.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Aggregate production:
        \begin{equation*}
          y_t=\left(\frac{k_t}{\Gamma_t}\right)^\alpha n_t^{1-\alpha}.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Labor demand:
        \begin{equation*}
          w_t=(1-\alpha)\Psi_t\frac{y_t}{n_t}.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Capital Euler equation:
        \begin{equation*}
          \frac{1}{c_t}
          =E_t\left[
          \frac{\beta}{\Gamma_{t+1}c_{t+1}}
          \left(r_{t+1}^k+1-\delta\right)
          \right].
        \end{equation*}

  \item Resource constraint:
        \begin{equation*}
          c_t+i_t
          =y_t-\frac{\varphi}{2}
          \left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}-1\right)^2y_t.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Capital accumulation:
        \begin{equation*}
          k_{t+1}=\frac{1-\delta}{\Gamma_t}k_t+i_t.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Rotemberg pricing:
        \begin{align*}
          \varphi\left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}-1\right)
          \frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}
           & =(1-\theta)+\theta\Psi_t \\
           & \quad+\varphi E_t\left[
            \beta\frac{c_t}{c_{t+1}}
            \left(\frac{\pi_{t+1}}{\bar\pi}-1\right)
            \frac{\pi_{t+1}}{\bar\pi}
            \frac{y_{t+1}}{y_t}
            \right].
        \end{align*}

  \item Nominal-bond Euler equation:
        \begin{equation*}
          1=s_tr_tE_t\left[
          \beta\frac{c_t}
          {\Gamma_{t+1}c_{t+1}\pi_{t+1}}
          \right].
        \end{equation*}

  \item Monetary policy:
        \begin{equation*}
          r_t=r^*
          \left(\frac{\pi_t}{\bar\pi}\right)^{\phi_\pi}
          \left(\frac{y_t}{\bar y}\right)^{\phi_y}.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Technology growth:
        \begin{equation*}
          \widehat\gamma_t
          =\rho_\Gamma\widehat\gamma_{t-1}
          +\sigma_\Gamma\varepsilon_t^\Gamma.
        \end{equation*}

  \item Risk premium:
        \begin{equation*}
          s_t=(1-\rho_s)\bar s+\rho_s s_{t-1}
          +\sigma_s\varepsilon_t^s.
        \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
